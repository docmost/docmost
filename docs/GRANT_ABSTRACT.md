# Grant-Style Abstract

This project develops a local-first archival system designed to preserve personal, community, and historical digital records in a transparent, durable, and reproducible manner. Current systems often rely on cloud infrastructure, proprietary platforms, or opaque AI workflows that can fail, disappear, or limit user control. Our approach centers on preserving raw source material in pristine form, creating working copies for indexing and analysis, and documenting every transformation through version history and integrity logs. By keeping data local and accountable, the project reduces dependency on centralized systems while providing a resilient framework for preserving records, community memory, and evidence. The immediate application is the preservation of Wisconsin activism archives, personal recovery records, and legal documentation; the broader impact is a scalable model for community-controlled stewardship of digital history. This work advances local infrastructure, human oversight, and transparent data governance in a way that is accessible, ethical, and sustainable.
